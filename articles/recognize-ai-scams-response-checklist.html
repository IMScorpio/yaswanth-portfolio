How to Recognize AI Scams: Voice Cloning and a Response Checklist

By Yaswanth Battina · Published 25 October 2026

AI scams often use an old trick with a more convincing message: impersonation. A voice clone, edited video, or polished message can make an urgent request feel familiar. The safest response is to verify the request through a separate channel you already trust before sending money, sharing a code, or installing software.

Quick answer: pause; call the person or organization using a saved number or official website; ask a question the caller cannot prepare for; never pay by gift card or cryptocurrency because someone creates urgency; and report financial fraud quickly to your bank and local authorities.

Common warning signs

  • Pressure to act immediately or keep the request secret.
  • A new phone number, unexpected payment route, or request for a one-time passcode.
  • A familiar voice asking you to transfer money before you can call back.
  • A supposed support agent asking for remote access, passwords, or a security code.
  • A payment request that avoids a normal invoice or established process.

Any one sign can have innocent explanations; treat combinations of urgency, secrecy, and unusual payment as a reason to stop and verify.

Verify a family emergency

Hang up and call the family member using a number already in your contacts. If they do not answer, contact another trusted person. Families can agree on a private phrase, but do not rely on a phrase alone if an account or device may be compromised. Do not trust caller ID or a voice as proof of identity.

Verify a business or support message

Open the organization’s app or type its known website address yourself. Contact support through the listed channel, not a link or number in the unexpected message. Never share a password reset code with a caller. If a message asks you to install software, stop and ask a trusted technician through a separate channel.

If you already sent money or shared access

  1. Contact your bank, card provider, or payment service immediately and ask whether the transaction can be stopped.
  2. Change affected passwords from a trusted device and enable multi-factor authentication.
  3. Preserve messages, account names, transaction IDs, and dates.
  4. Report through your country’s official fraud or cybercrime channel and follow local police guidance.

In India, the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal lists cybercrime.gov.in and helpline 1930 for reporting online financial fraud. In the United States, report scams to the Federal Trade Commission. Elsewhere, use your national consumer protection or police service’s official site.

Do this now: save your bank’s verified fraud number and agree on a callback habit with family. Share this checklist with someone who may receive urgent payment requests.

Sources: FTC guidance on AI-enhanced family emergency scams and India’s National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal. Procedures vary by country.
